Mobility overview

Current contract-relax rule: move under control to the edge of the stretch. Contract out against the position for 3–5 sec, then contract in toward deeper active range for 3–5 sec. Normal effort is 7–9/10. The left hamstring remains capped at 5–6/10 until symptom-stable and deliberately reviewed.

Saturday track preferred · once weekly · quality before fatigue

Weekend track power + plyometrics

The consistency goal is one weekly high-speed power exposure—not both a sprint workout and a full plyometric session. Saturday at the track supplies the true jump, rebound and landing work that is difficult to perform at Equinox. Wednesday remains available for occasional sprint workouts.

Weekly choice: if you complete a Wednesday sprint workout and the hamstring is ready for that stage, it counts as the week’s power exposure and Saturday plyometrics are skipped. Otherwise, perform Track A at the track on Saturday. Do not move it to Sunday before Monday legs.

Track A · Saturday track plyometrics

  1. 0:00–5:00 · Prepare: brisk walk or easy jog, ankle rocks, marching and two gradual skips. Everything must be pain-free.
  2. 5:00–7:00 · Snap-down to stick: 2 × 3. Rise tall, drop quickly into a shallow athletic stance, and freeze with quiet feet and knees tracking over the middle toes.
  3. 7:00–11:00 · Low pogo hops: 2 × 8 with 45–60 sec rest. Stay tall, rebound from the ankles and keep every contact quiet and low.
  4. 11:00–17:00 · Vertical jump to stick: 3 × 3 with 60–75 sec rest. Jump explosively without chasing maximum height; land softly and hold for two seconds.
  5. Finish there. Do not add bounds, depth jumps or conditioning intervals to fill the clock.

Track B · Occasional Wednesday sprint workout

  1. Use only after pain-free jogging and all preceding submaximal running stages are stable during the session and the following morning.
  2. 0:00–6:00 · Prepare: easy jog or bike, leg-cycle drills, A-skips and B-skips—all pain-free.
  3. 6:00–9:00 · Jump primer: snap-down to stick 1 × 3, then vertical jump to stick 2 × 3.
  4. 9:00–18:00 · Accelerations: 4–6 × 10–20 m with a full 90–120 sec walk-back. Begin at 60–70%; do not turn these into all-out sprints.
  5. Track B replaces Saturday’s Track A that week. Do not add extra jumps after sprinting.

Progression and stop rules

  • Start once weekly. Add repetitions only while every take-off and landing remains crisp.
  • Progress jump intent before adding jump count. Do not add depth jumps, repeated broad jumps or bounds yet.
  • For running above 80% speed, use small increases of about 5% rather than jumping directly to maximal sprinting.
  • Sprinting requires zero hamstring pain. Stop for a pull, pinch, sharp pain, protective stride change, unstable landing, or symptoms above baseline later that day or the next morning.
  • If the left hip or hamstring is irritable during the warm-up, omit the track work. A short seated Assault Bike session may be used another day only if it is symptom-free.

Power-quality rule: this is neural and technical practice, not a calorie challenge. End the block when speed, jump height, landing quality or bike cadence meaningfully drops—even if the planned repetitions are unfinished.

Background: ACSM describes gradually progressed jump and power training as useful for older adults. A Journal of Athletic Training hamstring-rehabilitation review supports completing progressive submaximal running before sprint exposure and using especially small speed increases above 80%.

Handstand Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday · Planche Tuesday, Wednesday and Saturday · Pull skill Thursday

Weekly skill priorities

Handstand · 5–8 min

  • All kick-ups use the right leg only.
  • 2–3 wall kick-ups, 2–3 chest-to-wall holds of 10–20 sec, then 3–5 crisp freestanding attempts.
  • Push tall, ribs down, glutes tight, use fingertip pressure, and stop before shaking.
  • Tuesday: perform handstands before planche. Wednesday: keep the longer handstand-practice day.
  • Friday: 2–3 attempts total before lifting to protect military press performance and preserve freshness for Saturday planche.
  • Monday and Sunday have no planned handstand work. If a Saturday class includes meaningful handstand practice, it replaces Friday’s exposure rather than adding a fourth day.

Parallette planche · Tuesday, Wednesday + Saturday

  • Tuesday technique: slow planche walks · 2 passes; planche leans · 2 × 10–15 sec; band-assisted tuck planche · 2 × 10–15 sec. Keep every set submaximal.
  • Wednesday microdose: after handstands, complete 2 easy planche leans of 8–10 sec and 2 band-assisted tuck holds of 8–10 sec at no more than 6/10 effort. This is practice, not a second hard session.
  • Saturday max effort: use the established serious planche session with full recovery between hard attempts. End the session when body line, elbow position, or scapular protraction clearly deteriorates.
  • Skip Wednesday’s microdose if Tuesday causes wrist or elbow stiffness. Do not train planche on Friday; Monday remains legs-only, and Thursday is reserved for pulling skills.
  • If wrists ache, cut walk passes first. If the elbow reacts, cut lean duration first.

Thursday pulling · 15–20 min

  • Muscle-up emphasis week: 4–6 crisp singles, then 2 light assisted one-arm pull-up sets per arm.
  • One-arm emphasis week: assisted one-arm pull-ups · 3–4 × 2–4 per arm, then 3 easy muscle-up singles.
  • Use enough assistance to keep the scapula controlled and both sides technically consistent. Rest 2–3 min between demanding sets.
  • Do not add maximal one-arm negatives. Stop for pain above 2/10, increased nerve symptoms, or next-morning elbow stiffness.

Block D cut rule: when the skill block expands the session, finishers are the first and official cut. Do not steal time from the heavy main lift, interleaved abs, or mobility and rehab blocks.

Weekday target · about 90 min

Lifting split

Session map: 0:00–0:10 warm-up · 0:10–0:25 skill block · 0:25–0:45 Block A · 0:45–1:05 Block B · 1:05–1:20 Block C · final 10 min for Block D or scheduled mobility. Monday omits the skill block; use that time for full recovery between leg sets instead of adding more work.

How the blocks and ab rounds work

  • Run each block as a superset or tri-set: move from A1 to A2 (or B1 to B2) before resting.
  • Block A pairs the heavy main lift only with a low-fatigue skill or recovery drill. Rest 75–90 sec after the full pairing.
  • Blocks B and C use non-competing movements. Rest 45–60 sec only after the complete pairing.
  • Thursday exception: Block B is intentionally a single one-arm machine-row slot after the pull-skill block and weighted pull-ups. Do not add lat pulldowns.
  • Tuesday and Thursday: complete Abs Round 1 after Block A, Round 2 after Block B, and Round 3 after Block C.
  • Block D is optional and always gets cut first when time is short.

Cardio + power coordination

Cardio options and sprint return

  • Target two aerobic sessions per week: Tuesday or Thursday evening plus a weekend session. Cycling remains the default, but one ride may be replaced by the StairMaster option below.
  • Until the hamstring is fully symptom-stable, avoid out-of-saddle sprints and hard standing climbs.
  • Sunday evening is Zone 2 only because Monday is leg day.
  • Saturday at the track is the preferred plyometric window. A Wednesday sprint workout replaces that Saturday session for the week.
  • Return to sprinting through pain-free drills, submaximal running, 60–70% accelerations and then gradual speed exposure. Do not skip stages because the bike feels strong.
  • Cycling is additive cardio and does not replace leg day or the planned hamstring exposures.

Zone 2 replacement

StairMaster · 25–40 min

  • Use this instead of one planned ride, preferably Tuesday or Thursday—not in addition to it.
  • After a 5-minute easy ramp, hold a steady conversational pace. You should be able to speak in full sentences without gasping.
  • Stay tall with light hands on the rails. Reduce the level if you need to lean forward or support your body weight with your arms.
  • Choose cycling instead if the StairMaster increases front-of-hip pinching, lower-back tightness, or next-day leg fatigue.

Occasional HIIT replacement

Assault Bike · about 15–20 min

  • Use this instead of one cardio session or the week’s indoor power fallback. Do not combine it with StairMaster Zone 2 or add it after track sprints.
  • Warm up: 5 min easy, gradually increasing cadence.
  • Intervals: 4–6 rounds of 15 sec hard at about 8/10 effort, followed by 75–90 sec very easy.
  • Cool down: 4–5 min easy. Stay seated and stop if hip, hamstring, back, or nerve symptoms increase.

Replacement rule: choose the planned ride, StairMaster Zone 2, or Assault Bike HIIT for that cardio slot—only one. The HIIT option is not recovery work and should not be placed on Friday before Saturday’s max-effort planche and track work.

Tempo 3–1–1

Three sec lowering, one sec pause, one sec lifting.

Zone 2

Easy aerobic work at a sustainable conversational effort.

Plyometric

A rapid jump, hop or bound using a quick loading-and-rebound action. Assault Bike bursts and sprints are power work, but only the jump portion of this plan is plyometric.

Weekly review

Progression checkpoints

  • Track the next-day left-hamstring response to Mobility A, especially the half-kneeling hamstring hinge.
  • Track 90/90 lift-off quality and the left hip response to internal-rotation work.
  • Track morning-after elbow stiffness following planche leans and pressing.
  • Track whether three planned handstand exposures plus Tuesday technique, Wednesday’s easy planche microdose, and Saturday max-effort planche improve hold quality, freshness, and next-morning wrist and elbow response.
  • Track Thursday pulling quality: clean muscle-up singles, one-arm assistance level, left/right consistency, and the next-morning elbow and nerve response.
  • Track the week’s power quality: Saturday contact quietness and jump consistency, or Wednesday acceleration speed, plus the hip/hamstring response later that day and the next morning.
  • The Aug 9 checkpoint is available for review: progress the left hamstring toward 7–9/10 contractions only if pain remains 0–2/10 with no next-day spike.
  • After Aug 16, consider left-forward half-split negatives and gradual stride reintroduction if symptom-stable.
  • Strength progression requires crisp sets and a quiet next day: add 1–2 reps or the smallest useful load increase, never both at once.
