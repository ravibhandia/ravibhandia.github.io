Upper-body preparation

Current warm-up routine

A short sequence for tendon comfort, gentle neural mobility, shoulder extension, and scapular elevation before upper-body training.

Keep this easy. Nerve glides are smooth sliders, not maximal stretches. Stop a movement if it causes sharp pain, increasing numbness or tingling, weakness, or symptoms that remain after the session. The towel and bar movements should follow the range and dosage already cleared for the tendon.

Step 01 · Tendon preparation

Behind-the-back towel assistance

This is the towel position currently used to make the tendon movement more comfortable. The towel assists the range; it does not force it.

EquipmentLong towel or strap

Setup: Hold the towel behind the back in the exact hand position already prescribed. Stand tall with the ribs stacked and both shoulders relaxed.

  • Use the opposite hand only to take up slack and guide the affected side.
  • Move slowly through a comfortable range; do not yank the towel or chase a deeper shoulder stretch.
  • Dose: keep the current clinician-prescribed hold or repetition scheme.

Step 02 · Neural mobility

Three upper-limb nerve glides

Move between an easier and a slightly tensioned position, then return. Stay relaxed and never hold a nerve glide at a strong endpoint.

01 · Palm-forward pattern

Median nerve glide

Keep the shoulder down. Open the palm and slowly lengthen the elbow as the wrist extends, then soften the elbow and wrist to return.

  • Think smooth “out and back,” not a sustained stretch.
  • Reduce wrist or elbow range first if symptoms increase.

02 · Hand-to-face pattern

Ulnar nerve glide

Begin with the arm out and palm down. Extend the wrist gently, then bend the elbow so the hand travels toward the side of the face; reverse to return.

  • Keep the shoulder relaxed instead of hiking it up.
  • A mild pull can be acceptable; pain or worsening symptoms are not.

03 · Thumb-in pattern

Radial nerve glide

Drop the shoulder gently, turn the arm inward with the wrist flexed, and move the straight arm slightly behind and away; reverse the sequence to return.

  • Use a small range and keep the neck relaxed.
  • Do not force the wrist or pull the shoulder aggressively downward.

Step 03 · Shoulder extension

Tall-kneeling banded shoulder extensions

This is the established daily shoulder-extension prescription from the training plan.

Dose2 × 8–10
+ 1 hold

Setup: Anchor a light band in front at chest height. Kneel tall facing the anchor, hold the band with straight elbows, and brace the glutes and abdomen.

  • Pull the straight arms behind the torso for 2 seconds.
  • Hold the comfortable end range for 2 seconds.
  • Return under control for 3 seconds.
  • After the repetitions, complete one comfortable 15–20 second end-range hold.

Step 04 · Scapular elevation

Behind-the-back Olympic-bar shrugs

Finish with the empty bar behind the body to practice a controlled up-and-slightly-back shrug.

LoadEmpty Olympic bar

Setup: Stand tall with the bar behind the thighs, hands just outside the hips, elbows straight, and ribs stacked.

  • Lift the shoulders up and slightly back without bending the elbows or leaning forward.
  • Pause briefly, then lower the shoulders slowly instead of dropping the bar.
  • Keep the bar close without forcing the shoulders into an uncomfortable behind-the-back position.
  • Dose: use the current prescribed sets and repetitions; do not add plates unless that progression has been cleared.

Clinical reference: Hospital guidance consistently treats nerve glides as gentle, relaxed movement and advises reducing or stopping them if symptoms worsen or persist. Your individual therapist’s version and dosage take priority over this summary.
