Supporting guide

C5/C6 nerve mobility

Why the three upper-limb nerve glides are in the routine, how to keep them gentle, and where strict towel-supported shoulder rotation fits.

Updated August 15, 2026 · General education, not a diagnosis or replacement for individual clinical advice

The key distinction: C5 and C6 are nerve roots in the neck. Median, radial, and ulnar nerves are peripheral nerves that continue into the arm. The three glide patterns are not one-to-one “C5” or “C6” exercises; they move different parts of the upper-limb neural pathway.

Purpose

Why nerve glides are in this routine

Current context

This routine records a history of C5/C6-related neck-to-arm symptoms. The glides are included as gentle neural-mobility work to help the nerves move smoothly while the neck, shoulder, elbow, and wrist move.

What they may help

When appropriately selected, relaxed slider movements may help manage neural sensitivity and maintain comfortable movement. They should feel like motion—not a hard stretch or strength exercise.

What they do not do

Nerve glides do not guarantee prevention of future symptoms, remove structural compression, or replace assessment when numbness or weakness is changing.

Technique rules

Use sliders, not sustained stretches

  1. 01

    Stay relaxed. Keep the neck, jaw, and shoulder easy rather than bracing.

  2. 02

    Move slowly out and back. One end of the pathway lengthens as the other softens.

  3. 03

    Use the prescribed pattern and dose. More range or more repetitions are not automatically better.

  4. 04

    Symptoms must settle promptly. Reduce the range or stop if tingling, numbness, or pain builds or remains afterward.

Current warm-up

The three upper-limb patterns

Palm-forward pattern

Median nerve glide

Open the palm and gradually lengthen the elbow as the wrist extends, then soften the elbow and wrist to return.

Hand-to-face pattern

Ulnar nerve glide

Move the hand toward the side of the face as the elbow bends, then reverse the sequence smoothly.

Thumb-in pattern

Radial nerve glide

Use a small inward arm turn and wrist-flexed position as the arm moves slightly behind and away, then return.

Use only the pattern and range that your clinician has selected. Symptom location alone cannot reliably identify a specific cervical nerve root or peripheral nerve.

Separate strengthening exercise

Towel-supported band external rotation

This strengthens the shoulder external rotators while the towel gives immediate feedback that the elbow is staying fixed. It supports shoulder control; it is not a C5/C6 nerve glide or a decompression exercise.

Equipment:Light band and small towel
Dose:1–2 × 8–12 per side

Setup: Anchor the band at elbow height. Stand tall with the working elbow bent 90 degrees and place a folded towel between the elbow and the side of the ribs, just above the hip.

  • Movement: Keep the elbow gently pinching the towel and rotate the hand outward against the band. Pause briefly, then return slowly.
  • Key cue: The elbow stays in one place; only the upper arm rotates in the shoulder socket.
  • Avoid: Do not twist the torso, hike the shoulder, flare the ribs, bend the wrist, or squeeze the towel maximally.
  • Adjust: Use a lighter band or smaller range if the elbow drifts or the neck and upper trapezius take over.
  • Stop if: The movement causes sharp shoulder pain, increasing arm symptoms, numbness, or weakness.

When to stop and get reviewed

Stop the glides and seek clinical advice for worsening or persistent numbness, new weakness, loss of grip or hand coordination, or symptoms that are no longer settling after the exercise. Seek urgent assessment for major balance changes, rapidly progressive weakness, or new bladder or bowel changes.
